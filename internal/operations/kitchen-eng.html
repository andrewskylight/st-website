High Level Picture

The most important thing for the chef is guests' lunch. Followed by crew food, then shopping.

Chef's tasks throughout the day can be separated like so:

  • Morning Setups
  • Lunch for Guests and Crew
  • Prepare Crew Dinner
  • Shopping (if needed)

An experienced chef will create some overlap between tasks.

Daily Kitchen Operating Procedure

Author: Cristian

Morning Routine

Before preparing breakfast, place two packages of chicken breast to thaw so they can later be boiled. At the same time, start cooking the beans, as both require considerable cooking time. Once both are underway, begin preparing breakfast for the crew. Breakfast should be served between 7:30 a.m. and 8:30 a.m.; whenever possible, avoid serving it too late, as it is difficult for the crew to begin working immediately after eating. After breakfast: wash all utensils, clean and sanitize the galley, and organize the workspace before starting lunch preparation.

Lunch Preparation

Prepare a basic sofrito using onion, garlic, and bell peppers. Cook everything in one pot and then divide it into two portions: one for the chicken tomato sauce, one for the rice and beans.

Vegan Meals

At the beginning of the day, confirm during the crew briefing whether any crew members follow a vegan diet. If there are vegan crew members: set aside part of the sofrito before adding any animal products, and use this portion to prepare the tofu. Tofu has a very mild flavor, so the tomato sauce provides most of the taste and is generally well received. Always check that enough tofu is available.

If tofu is unavailable, or if there are more than five vegan portions required, prepare an alternative consisting of boiled potatoes, carrots, and sautéed vegetables — finish with a traditional Cuban-style garlic mojo sauce, which is usually very popular.

Chicken Preparation

The prepared sofrito is divided into two portions. One portion is used to make the chicken tomato sauce by adding two cans of tomato paste. The remaining portion is reserved for the rice and beans.

Rice Preparation

Measure five pounds of rice using the designated wooden measuring container, which is already marked with the correct measurement.

Additional Dietary Preferences

Keep in mind that some crew members eat fish but not meat — for this reason, always try to keep canned tuna available as an additional meal option.

Workflow During the Tour

Whenever sea conditions allow, begin cooking the rice during the first stop, as the boat is generally more stable at that time. While traveling to the second stop, prepare a separate meat dish for the crew, and wash and cut all vegetables needed for lunch service. Upon arriving at the second stop, there are approximately 35 minutes to finish cooking, plate and garnish the meals, set up the serving table, and prepare for lunch service. The objective is to have lunch completely ready to be served at 12:00 p.m., immediately after the second stop.

Although schedules may occasionally change due to weather or operational needs, this should always remain the target.

Dinner Preparation

As a best practice, prepare dinner while making lunch whenever possible. This allows the team to return to the marina with most of the work already completed, leaving only the need to reheat and serve dinner. This approach greatly reduces the workload at the end of the day, especially after working in high temperatures, completing post-tour cleaning, and making grocery runs, while also ensuring the crew does not have dinner too late in the evening.

Chef's Priority Learning Progression

The guide lists all the tasks the chef is to do during the day. These tasks are what the chef is to focus on as they progress.

  1. Guest Lunch & Kitchen Cleanliness (once that is mastered)
  2. Crew Dinner (once that is mastered)
  3. Crew Lunch (once that is mastered)
  4. Shopping (once that is mastered)
  5. Music — taking care of the music throughout the day (non-cooking task) (once that is mastered)
  6. Customer Experience — when a customer says "Oh, that was great," reply: "Thank you." Then slowly progress to having a 2–3 sentence conversation.

Discussion — Q&A

Crew Breakfast

It has to be good, but not fancy. Boiled eggs are good; Fabergé eggs are too much. People are to be fed, but not spoiled. If a crew member wants to prepare their own breakfast, that is fine — they are responsible for cleaning up after themselves.

Can we try other meats for the tour?

Chicken is a winner. Least religious restrictions. Least dietary restrictions. Mixing in pork did not work — you come across religions that don't eat pork.

Crew Lunch and Dinner

The priority is guest lunch. Crew needs to be fed. Food needs to be varied. Budget needs to be respected. A chef is to care for the crew and their well-being. Their food is to be adequate. However, the chef is not there to entertain the crew's taste preferences. The middle ground works best.
